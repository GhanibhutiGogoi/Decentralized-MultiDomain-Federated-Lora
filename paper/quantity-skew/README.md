# Quantity-skew decentralized LoRA paper

**Adaptive-Rank Decentralized LoRA under Quantity Skew: An SST-2 Benchmark** is a standalone paper about the completed RoBERTa-base/SST-2 comparison. It contains its own methodology, results, references, and four figure sets. It has no manuscript dependency on the original study and includes no original-study results or tables.

`main.tex` is the source; `main.pdf` is the delivery PDF compiled and visually reviewed on gpu003. The methodology defines the complete adaptive controller, including menus, capability floors, two-round warmup, demand and quality smoothing, hysteresis, restoration, and the zero-gradient convention. It also defines static sample weights, reversible neighbor mixing, factor/product aggregation, compact QR/SVD scaling, root assembly, payload accounting, endpoints, paired intervals, and factorial effects.

The completed experiment contains seven methods at five matched seeds, plus one separate equal-size Dec-LoRA reconstruction anchor. Adaptive/sample obtains **94.243 ± 0.249%** best labeled-validation accuracy, versus **94.541 ± 0.238%** for rank 16 and **94.495 ± 0.334%** for feasible rank 4. Its paired difference from rank 16 is −0.298 percentage points with an unadjusted 95% interval of [−0.755, +0.159]; this does not establish equivalence. Training traffic is **31.81% lower than rank 16 but 8.92% higher than rank 4**. The paper reports the unfavorable final-round endpoint and all factorial effects.

The conclusion is a completed negative comparison: the tested combination does not demonstrate superiority, established parity, or a better feasible accuracy/traffic tradeoff. The baseline is an independent reconstruction, not an exact reproduction or a claimed win over the publication's printed score. Official labeled validation, repeated best-round selection, single-process transport simulation, adapter-only capacity limits, and the absence of a privacy guarantee are explicit limitations. No pooled RoBERTa or hidden-test result is claimed.
