\begin{abstract}
Federated fine-tuning with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) must handle statistical heterogeneity, unequal device budgets, and unreliable central coordination at the same time. We present AH-LoRA, a framework whose implemented core aggregates LoRA adapters in effective-update space rather than factor space. A client sends $\dW_i=(\alpha/r_i)B_iA_i$ with a shared scaling constant $\alpha$; neighbors mix these matrices and each receiver refactorizes the result to its own rank by truncated SVD. This operation is invariant to the LoRA factor gauge and supports heterogeneous ranks in a serverless graph. We implement Metropolis--Hastings gossip, an oracle two-tier hierarchy, heterogeneous-rank and error-feedback protocols, direction-aware signatures, online domain discovery, and an adaptive-rank controller. On a real CIFAR-100 benchmark with 15 clients, five known domains, frozen ImageNet ResNet-18 features, 50 rounds, and three seeds, the oracle hierarchy reaches $68.98\pm0.95\%$ personalized accuracy at uniform rank 16, compared with $42.30\pm1.39\%$ for flat gossip and $23.20\pm0.29\%$ for centralized FedAvg under the same personalized evaluation. The online discovery mixer infers five groups with stage-10 scoring ARI $1.00$ for all three seeds (stage-20 ARI $1.00/0.918/1.00$ under coordinator-visible full-state observation). A matched 30-round heterogeneous-rank ablation shows the integrated loss-adaptive rank policy at $20.30\pm2.51\%$ versus $24.16\pm1.04\%$ for fixed-rank MH; bounded domain reweighting produces no measurable change in this run. A revised controller reaches parity with a feasible Fashion-MNIST baseline while saving 10.0\% FLOPs, and no privacy guarantee is measured. Thus the project does not yet satisfy its original end-to-end objective; the integrated ablation is negative for the tested rank policy and does not validate a privacy benefit.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
Large pretrained models are commonly specialized with LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora}, which trains small factors $B\in\R^{d_o\times r}$ and $A\in\R^{r\times d_i}$ while freezing the backbone. Existing federated LoRA methods address parameter efficiency and, in some cases, rank heterogeneity~\citep{zhang2023fedit,babakniya2023slora,cho2024hetlora,bai2024flexlora}; decentralized optimization provides serverless gossip protocols~\citep{boyd2006gossip,lian2017dpsgd}. These lines of work leave a practical intersection under-specified: clients may have different ranks, different domains, and no trusted aggregation server.

LoRA factors are not identifiable: $(BQ,Q^{-1}A)$ represents the same update as $(B,A)$ for every invertible $Q$. Factor averaging therefore depends on arbitrary coordinates and is undefined for unequal ranks. AH-LoRA defines the communicated object in the common matrix space $\dW_i=(\alpha/r_i)B_iA_i$ with shared $\alpha$ and refactorizes every mixed update with an SVD. This primitive is local, rank-agnostic, and compatible with symmetric doubly-stochastic mixing.

Our contribution is deliberately scoped: a tested effective-update merge and Metropolis--Hastings gossip implementation; a reproducible CIFAR-100 benchmark; heterogeneous-rank, factor zero-padding, and error-feedback ablations; offline and online direction-aware discovery; an auditable adaptive-rank controller; and a real-data regeneration of the Project 2 calibration pipeline. Neighborhood-local discovery, calibrated rank adaptation, privacy guarantees, and broad multimodal generalization remain open.
\section{Problem Formulation}
Let clients $i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$ communicate over an undirected graph $G$. Client $i$ owns private data from domain $d(i)$ and maintains a LoRA update $\dW_i$. Ranks $r_i$ may differ but must satisfy local resource ceilings. A symmetric doubly-stochastic mixing matrix $W$ preserves the network mean. We report personalized accuracy (each client on its held-out shard) and consensus accuracy (a reference-rank update on the union of test shards), plus worst-domain accuracy, the best-minus-worst domain gap, consensus distance, residual energy, and effective versus operational communication counts.
The idealized objective is
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\{\dW_i\}} F=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N f_i(W_0+\dW_i)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \operatorname{rank}(\dW_i)\le r_i,\; (i,j)\in E\text{ for every transmitted pair}.
\end{equation}
The reported personalized score is $A_{\rm pers}=N^{-1}\sum_i\operatorname{Acc}_i(\mathcal T_i)$; the consensus adapter is $\dW_{\rm con}=\mathcal C_R(N^{-1}\sum_i\dW_i)$ and the fairness gap is $\max_d A_d-\min_d A_d$.
\section{Method}
AH-LoRA combines three components that can be evaluated independently and then composed: domain-aware weighting (Project 2), gauge-invariant decentralized aggregation (Project 3), and stateful rank selection (Project 1). The following subsections define the operators used in the experiments and make explicit which quantities are observed, communicated, and optimized.

\subsection{Domain-aware aggregation weights}
For client $i$, let $n_i$ denote the local sample count, $\ell_i$ its measured local loss, $q_i=(1+\ell_i)^{-1}$ its quality score, and $\lambda_i$ its domain-aware multiplier. Project 2 normalizes the contribution used by the weighted aggregator as
\begin{equation}
 a_i=\frac{n_i q_i\lambda_i}{\sum_j n_j q_j\lambda_j},\qquad \sum_i a_i=1.
\end{equation}
The fitted quality score is linear in standardized measurements, $s_i=\theta_0+\theta^\top x_i$. Within each task/round group $g$, the multiplier is centered before exponentiation and then clipped and renormalized:
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_i=\operatorname{renorm}_{[0.5,1.5],\,\mathrm{mean}=1}\!\left(\exp\left[\gamma\left(s_i-\overline{s}_{g}\right)\right]\right),
 \qquad \overline{s}_{g}=|g|^{-1}\sum_{j\in g}s_j.
\end{equation}
The standardized calibration estimates its coefficients by ridge regression,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\theta_{\alpha}=\arg\min_{\theta}\left\{\lVert X\theta-y\rVert_2^2+\alpha\lVert\theta\rVert_2^2\right\},\qquad
 \widehat\theta_{\alpha}=(X^\top X+\alpha I)^{-1}X^\top y,
\end{equation}
with $\alpha$ selected by leave-one-task-out RMSE. The two calibration forms differ only in the components of $x_i$; both are treated as exploratory when predictive fit is weak.

The conservative follow-up uses no fitted coefficients. Let $p_i=n_iq_i/\sum_j n_jq_j$, let $z_{ik}$ be feature $k$ standardized across clients and clipped to $[-3,3]$, and set $s_i=K^{-1}\sum_k z_{ik}$. With temperature $T=1$, blend $\eta=0.10$, and bound $\delta=0.15$, it computes
\begin{align}
 e_i&=\frac{\exp(\operatorname{clip}(s_i/T,-4,4))}{\sum_jp_j\exp(\operatorname{clip}(s_j/T,-4,4))},\\
 h_i&=1+\eta(e_i-1),\qquad \lambda_i=\operatorname{clip}(c h_i,1-\delta,1+\delta),\quad \sum_ip_i\lambda_i=1.
\end{align}
The positive scalar $c$ is found by bisection. Constant features or zero blend return $\lambda_i=1$. This preserves the total base weight while bounding its redistribution; the reported follow-up evaluates contribution ranking, not retraining accuracy.

The measured domain signals begin with class proportions $p_{ic}=n_{ic}/\sum_c n_{ic}$. We record entropy, normalized entropy, and an imbalance statistic,
\begin{equation}
 H_i=-\sum_{c:p_{ic}>0}p_{ic}\log p_{ic},\quad H_i^{\rm norm}=H_i/\log C,\quad B_i=\frac{\max_c n_{ic}}{\min_{c:n_{ic}>0}n_{ic}}\left(1+\frac{z_i}{C}\right),
\end{equation}
where $z_i$ counts absent classes. With global proportions $q_c$ and $m_c=(p_{ic}+q_c)/2$, the recorded divergences are
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{KL}(p_i\|q)=\sum_c p_{ic}\log\frac{p_{ic}+\varepsilon}{q_c+\varepsilon},\qquad
 \operatorname{JS}(p_i,q)=\tfrac12\operatorname{KL}(p_i\|m)+\tfrac12\operatorname{KL}(q\|m),
\end{equation}
and update-distance features are $d_{\cos}(u,\bar u)=1-u^\top\bar u/(\|u\|_2\|\bar u\|_2)$ and $d_2(u,\bar u)=\|u-\bar u\|_2$. The weighted rank-assignment variant combines normalized signals as
\begin{equation}
 u_i=\lambda_c C_i+\lambda_e E_i+\lambda_b B_i,\qquad
 \widetilde r_i=r_{\min}+\frac{u_i}{\sum_j u_j}\left(B_{\rm tot}-Nr_{\min}\right),
\end{equation}
then snaps to the allowed menu and greedily corrects to the total rank budget; if $\sum_j u_j=0$, uniform weights are used.

\subsection{Gauge-invariant effective-update aggregation}
A LoRA adapter with factors $B_i\in\mathbb R^{d_o\times r_i}$ and $A_i\in\mathbb R^{r_i\times d_i}$ communicates the scaled effective update using a shared scaling constant $\alpha$
\begin{equation}
 \dW_i=\frac{\alpha}{r_i}B_iA_i.
\end{equation}
The P3 runner and all heterogeneous-rank comparisons use this scaled update; the legacy Project 1 FedAvg path retains its historical unscaled $B_iA_i$ aggregation and is reported separately. For neighbors $\mathcal N(i)$, client $i$ forms a convex update-space merge,
\begin{equation}
 \overline{\dW}_i=\sum_{j\in\mathcal N(i)\cup\{i\}}w_{ij}\dW_j,\qquad \sum_jw_{ij}=1,\;w_{ij}\ge0.
\end{equation}
If $\overline{\dW}_i=U\Sigma V^\top$, its rank-$r_i$ representation is obtained from the truncated SVD:
\begin{align}
 \mathcal C_{r_i}(\overline{\dW}_i)&=U_{(:,1:r_i)}\Sigma_{1:r_i}V_{(:,1:r_i)}^\top,\nonumber\\
 B_i&=\frac{U_{(:,1:r_i)}\Sigma_{1:r_i}^{1/2}}{\sqrt{\alpha/r_i}},\qquad
 A_i=\frac{\Sigma_{1:r_i}^{1/2}V_{(:,1:r_i)}^\top}{\sqrt{\alpha/r_i}}.
\end{align}
The projection is the Eckart--Young minimum-Frobenius-error rank-$r_i$ approximation. It is invariant to the LoRA factor gauge: replacing $(B,A)$ by $(BQ,Q^{-1}A)$ leaves $BA$ unchanged, while entry-wise factor averaging changes in general and introduces cross terms.

\begin{proposition}[Factor averaging is gauge dependent]
Replacing one factor pair by $(BQ,Q^{-1}A)$ leaves $BA$ unchanged but changes entry-wise factor averages in general; expansion also introduces cross terms. Thus factor averaging is not a function of represented updates, whereas the $\dW$ average is.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Decentralized mixing, hierarchy, and feedback}
With a symmetric doubly-stochastic mixing matrix $W^t$, client $i$ receives
\begin{equation}
 Y_i^t=\sum_{j=1}^N W_{ij}^t\dW_j^t+m_i^t,\qquad \overline{\dW}_i^t=\mathcal C_{r_i}(Y_i^t),
\end{equation}
where $m_i^t=0$ without error feedback. The discarded residual is retained as
\begin{equation}
 m_i^{t+1}=Y_i^t-\mathcal C_{r_i}(Y_i^t).
\end{equation}
Flat gossip uses ring Metropolis--Hastings weights, $W_{ij}=1/(1+\max(d_i,d_j))$ on edges and $W_{ii}=1-\sum_{j\ne i}W_{ij}$. The oracle two-tier schedule is $W^t=W_{\mathrm{intra}}^tW_{\mathrm{bridge}}^tW_{\mathrm{intra}}^t$ on bridge rounds and $W^t=W_{\mathrm{intra}}^t$ otherwise. It uses known domains only as an oracle diagnostic.

Communication is reported in factor floats rather than matrix entries. If source $j$ has rank $r_j$ and layer dimensions $(d_{o,\ell},d_{i,\ell})$, its payload is $S_j=\sum_\ell r_j(d_{o,\ell}+d_{i,\ell})$ floats. For mixer support $E_t=\{(i,j):i\ne j,W^t_{ij}>0\}$, the effective count is
\begin{equation}
 F_t=\sum_{(i,j)\in E_t}S_j.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Automatic discovery and adaptive mixing}
Online discovery compresses the flattened effective updates using a fixed seeded projection $P\in\mathbb R^{64\times D}$. The normalized signature and its exponential moving average are
\begin{equation}
 z_i^t=\frac{P\operatorname{vec}(\dW_i^t)}{\left\lVert P\operatorname{vec}(\dW_i^t)\right\rVert_2},\qquad h_i^t=\frac{\beta h_i^{t-1}+(1-\beta)z_i^t}{\left\lVert\beta h_i^{t-1}+(1-\beta)z_i^t\right\rVert_2}.
\end{equation}
The affinity is $A_{ij}=h_i^\top h_j$. On the selected topology, we construct a nonnegative kernel and project it to a symmetric mixer,
\begin{equation}
 K_{ij}=\mathbf 1\{j\in\mathcal N(i)\cup\{i\}\}\exp\left(\frac{A_{ij}-A_{\max}}{\tau}\right),\quad W_0=\operatorname{Sinkhorn}(K),\quad W=w_{\min}I+(1-w_{\min})W_0.
\end{equation}
The unknown cluster count is selected without labels by $\widehat K=\arg\max_{k\in\{2,\ldots,\min(8,N-1)\}}\operatorname{Silhouette}(1-A,k)$. If all scores are non-positive, $\widehat K=1$ and the soft mixer is retained; labels and ARI/NMI are used only for evaluation.

\subsection{Adaptive rank selection}
Project 1 estimates the stable rank of a probe gradient $G_i$ and applies a capability floor:
\begin{equation}
 s_i=\frac{\lVert G_i\rVert_F^2}{\lVert G_i\rVert_2^2},\qquad c_i=\frac{\operatorname{index}(b_i)}{M-1},\qquad r_i^{\star}=q_{\mathcal R_i}\left(\max\left\{r_{\min},\gamma c_iR_i^{\max},\min(s_i,R_i^{\max})\right\}\right),
\end{equation}
where $q_{\mathcal R_i}$ snaps to the allowed candidate menu. At round $t$, optional residual demand $\rho_i^t$ modifies the signal as
\begin{equation}
 d_i^t=\operatorname{clip}\left(s_i^t\left[1+\omega\operatorname{clip}(\rho_i^t,0,1)\right],0,R_i^{\max}\right),\qquad \widetilde d_i^t=\beta_r\widetilde d_i^{t-1}+(1-\beta_r)d_i^t.
\end{equation}
The stateful controller changes rank only after the configured hysteresis margin and patience conditions are satisfied. This makes rank decisions auditable while allowing capability ceilings to be enforced at every client.

The signature experiment compares singular-value spectra, row-norm signatures, and inverse-$\dW$-L2 signatures at stages 2, 5, 10, and 20, scored against known labels offline. The benchmark uses uniform rank 16 or the repeating heterogeneous schedule $(4,12,32)$ with total rank budget 240 across 15 clients. Online discovery and adaptive-rank results are evaluated as separate extensions.
\section{Analysis}
For symmetric doubly-stochastic $W$, with $J=\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/N$, consensus error contracts as $\norm{(W-J)X}_F\le|\lambda_2(W)|\norm{(I-J)X}_F$. For rank-$R$ matrices, the SVD projection obeys $\norm{C_r(X)-X}_F^2=\sum_{k>r}\sigma_k(X)^2\le(1-r/R)\norm{X}_F^2$. With error feedback, the mean update follows the averaged update up to stochastic-gradient terms; without feedback, biased compression leaves an approximation floor. These statements require fixed mixing and bounded residual assumptions; the repository analysis does not claim convergence for the state-dependent discovery mixer or adaptive-rank controller.

\subsection{Why component gains do not automatically transfer}
The adaptive-rank and domain-weighting studies answer narrower questions than the decentralized benchmark. For client $i$ at round $t$, the end-to-end update is
\[
\dW_{i,t+1}=C_{r_{i,t}}\!\left(\sum_j W_{ij,t}\,\dW_{j,t}^{\mathrm{local}}\right),
\qquad \dW_{j,t}^{\mathrm{local}}=\frac{\alpha}{r_{j,t}}B_{j,t}A_{j,t},
\]
where both the mixing matrix $W_t$ and the projection rank $r_{i,t}$ affect the next local objective. The isolated Project~1 experiment changes $r_{i,t}$ on a single task and compares final task accuracy at matched client capability ceilings. The P3 battery instead fixes rank 16, or the repeating $(4,12,32)$ schedule, and evaluates personalized and consensus predictions after 50 gossip rounds. Since the controller is not called by the P3 runner, its parity result cannot be interpreted as an end-to-end P3 result.

The representation also differs: the Project~1/P2 Experiment~1 models apply the unscaled update $B_iA_i$, whereas P3's ResNet head applies $(\alpha/r_i)B_iA_i$ and compensates for this scale during refactorization. Each merge matches its own forward convention; no missing-scale error was found. These parameterizations nevertheless induce different local optimization dynamics.

Project~2 measures a different quantity. Its target is a leave-one-client-out contribution, $y_i=\mathrm{Acc}(\mathrm{all})-\mathrm{Acc}(\mathrm{all}\setminus i)$, and it evaluates whether a multiplier improves the ranking of $y_i$:
\[
\widetilde w_i=\frac{n_iq_i\lambda_i}{\sum_k n_kq_k\lambda_k}.
\]
The reported Spearman and pairwise gains therefore measure rank association with $y_i$ over 75 recorded rows. In the current training path these factors are not passed into the P3 aggregation loop, so they cannot change P3 personalized accuracy. Even when wired in, improving a contribution ranking need not improve each client's local loss: the P3 objective is a vector of personalized losses (and, separately, a consensus loss), whereas Project~2 optimizes a scalar leave-one-out proxy.

Thus the apparent discrepancy does not demonstrate an arithmetic error. A valid end-to-end attribution requires the same dataset split, seeds, rounds, rank budget, initialization, evaluation target, and communication graph, with one component toggled at a time. P3 controls these variables within its comparator set, but the current evidence does not isolate the joint composition of all three components; claims of adaptive-rank or domain-weighted gains in the full decentralized pipeline would therefore be unsupported.
\section{Experimental Setup}
\paragraph{Vision.} We use all 50,000 CIFAR-100 training images and 10,000 test images. A frozen ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 produces 512-dimensional features after deterministic resize to $224\times224$ and ImageNet normalization. The trainable classification head is a $100\times512$ LoRA update. Five superclass groups form domains, with three clients per domain and Dirichlet($0.5$) within-domain partitions. Runs use $\alpha=32$, one local epoch, learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch size 128, 50 rounds, and seeds 42, 43, and 44.
\paragraph{Comparators and evidence.} We compare local-only, centralized $\dW$ FedAvg, flat Metropolis--Hastings gossip, oracle two-tier gossip, factor zero-padding, and error feedback. Every run records manifests, split hashes, per-round metrics, per-domain metrics, and communication accounting. The separate Project 2 regeneration uses real CIFAR-10, FashionMNIST, AG News, UCI Heart Disease, and SpeechCommands data; Experiment 1 has 75 client-round rows at seed 42.
\paragraph{Adaptive-rank battery.} Project 1 compares fixed rank 32 with the shipped controller ($\gamma=0.5$) on five real tasks for five rounds at seed 42. Three clients use batch-size capabilities 16, 64, and 256, corresponding to maximum ranks 4, 8, and 16. The controller logs stable-rank demand, EMA state, hysteresis decisions, selected ranks, FLOPs, and capability violations.

\section{Limitations and Reproducibility}
The oracle hierarchy receives true domain assignments, and offline signature scores use those labels only for evaluation. The online discovery result is coordinator-visible because the current runner callback supplies all adapter states; a neighborhood-local discovery protocol and its communication overhead remain future work. The benchmark is one frozen-feature CIFAR-100 task; it does not establish behavior for LLMs, asynchronous churn, or other modalities. Communication is simulated payload accounting rather than measured network throughput. Three seeds provide variability estimates, not statistical proof. P2 calibration is a single-seed, five-round diagnostic with weak predictive fit. The original Project 1 controller failed the fixed rank-32 accuracy gate (rank 2 throughout, 93.75\% FLOP savings, accuracy loss on three tasks); that reference exceeds every client's capability maximum. The revised controller reaches final-accuracy parity with the feasible $(4,8,16)$ baseline on one Fashion-MNIST run at seed 42 with 10.0\% FLOP savings, and this has not been shown on the other four tasks. Conservative domain weighting improves model-free contribution ranking modestly (Spearman 0.152 to 0.196) without an end-to-end no-regression result. No privacy guarantee is demonstrated: low-rank adapters and decentralized exchange do not by themselves establish privacy, and differential-privacy, membership-inference, reconstruction, or secure-aggregation experiments remain to be run.

Remote validation used Python 3.10, PyTorch 2.3 with CUDA 12.1, and Tesla V100 GPUs; no credentials are stored in the artifact bundle.

\section{Conclusion}
AH-LoRA demonstrates a practical primitive for decentralized federated LoRA with heterogeneous ranks: mix scaled effective updates and refactorize locally. The completed benchmark shows that protocol choice and domain structure strongly affect personalized behavior, factor zero-padding can fail under rank heterogeneity, and error feedback changes the compression trade-off. Known-domain hierarchy is a useful oracle comparison, and the online extension demonstrates automatic recovery of the five CIFAR-100 groups under coordinator-visible observations. The matched integration ablation is negative for the tested loss-adaptive rank policy: it trails fixed MH by 3.85 percentage points, while bounded domain reweighting has no measurable effect. The revised adaptive-rank controller reaches final-accuracy parity with a feasible $(4,8,16)$ Fashion-MNIST baseline while saving 10.0\% FLOPs, but broad accuracy-preserving adaptive LoRA is still unestablished. Neither result provides a privacy guarantee. Consequently, the project is not successful against its original end-to-end criterion yet, although the failure is now measured under a composed protocol rather than inferred from mismatched component studies.
